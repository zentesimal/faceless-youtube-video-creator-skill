# Editorial

A clean, news-magazine explainer: big serif headlines on lots of white, red for the numbers that matter, a yellow highlighter on the key word. For topics carried by facts and data: how something works, what changed, what the numbers say.

## Source

Original to this skill (no reference video). Built from the classic print-editorial recipe: one serif display face, one sans for text, black on off-white, one signal colour.

## Look

Off-white ground, near-black text and dark slides, red (`main`) for numbers and bold, yellow (`accent`) for highlights. Fraunces for headlines, Inter for text. No texture, no logo, no progress bar; quiet "Chapter n" labels.

## Imagery source

- Real artifacts carry it: `table`, `doc`, `fields` and `number` slides with real data, sources and dates. This look is mostly proof.
- `scene` slides sparingly (one in eight), for the human moment in a story; generated illustrations, when on, in a minimal editorial style (`imageStyle`).

## Narration

- Lead with the question and the answer's headline: "Prices went up 40%. Here's who actually pays."
- One number or one source per idea, said plainly, with its date. Then what it means for the viewer.
- Short sentences, no jokes in the facts, a little dry wit in the bridges.

## Slides

- Pace 3–4. A `number` for every striking figure (with `from` for a change), a `table` for comparisons (≤ 6 rows), `statement` slides for the takeaways.
- One highlight per slide, on the word that carries the claim.
- Sources on screen: put the publisher and date in the slide's `note` or `sub`.

## Product screens

Real `screen` slides in a plain `window` or `browser` frame, no treatment; the source credit under the frame is the citation.

## Avoid

Unsourced numbers, charts drawn from memory, more than one signal colour per slide, decorative pictures.

# Changelog

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-21)

Covers for Shorts, and a developer audience.

- **A cover for every Short**: a click-driving 1080×1920 cover image, designed like the long video's thumbnails (a messaging angle, ≤ 4 words, one highlight, the mascot or a scene), for the Shorts shelf, a channel's Shorts tab and TikTok / Instagram grids. You see two covers on different angles, approve or give feedback (same review loop as the thumbnails), and pick the one that ships: `render-thumbnails.mjs` renders `cover-A.png` / `cover-B.png` when the spec (or the Short's `style.json`) has `"format": "short"`, and `--pick A` copies the winner to `cover.png`. The compare sheet shows each cover on the Shorts shelf, in a profile grid's 3:4 crop and small; text outside the cover's safe zone fails the render. Layouts: `bold`, `question`, `title`, `mood`, `card`.
- `youtube-meta.mjs` checks a Short's `cover` (exists, ≤ 2 MB, 9:16).
- **Developer audience**: the first question now offers `Developer (solo / indie)` next to the viewer levels. It saves `"audience": "developer"` with a level (4 by default) and changes the hooks, comparisons (from programming, not everyday life), proof (real code, terminal output, configs and costs on screen) and packaging, not the depth. `script-stats.mjs` stops counting software words (API, JSON, endpoint, SDK, …) as jargon for that audience, and warns when a developer video shows no code. See `references/script-playbook.md` › Audience: developer.

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-20)

Story teller mode.

- **Story teller mode** (`"mode": "storyteller"` in `style.json`, asked next to free or OpenAI): a generated illustration for every sentence. Each sentence gets its own spoken line and its own `illustration` cue; `script-stats.mjs` and the renderer check one sentence per picture and at most ~12 s per picture. The sentence sets the cut at any pace: pictures dissolve into each other, still pictures push in, and with animations on each picture takes the next camera move in turn (push, pan, drift, pull, rise). New `references/video-playbook.md` › Story teller mode and `script-playbook.md` › Writing for Story teller.
- **Recurring characters**: describe a person, place or object once in `style.json` `"characters"` and name it as `{Maya}` in any prompt; every picture that names it gets the same description, so it looks the same from picture to picture. An undescribed `{Name}` is caught before anything is billed.
- `gen-images.mjs` generates 4 pictures at a time (`--jobs`), defaults to `medium` quality in Story teller mode (`"imageQuality"` or `--quality` to change it), lists long batches compactly in `--dry-run` (`--full` for every prompt), stops early when the key, credit or network fails for every picture, and no longer prints the request (with the API key) when a request fails.

## 0.2.1 (2026-11-20)

- **Thumbnail review loop**: you see thumbnails A and B before delivery, approve each one or give feedback on it (words, picture or layout, angle, or free text), and the skill re-renders until both are approved. An approved variant stays as it is, so the A/B test stays fair. After 3 rounds on one variant it offers 3 fresh alternatives. Every round is kept in `thumbs/rounds/r<n>/` and the feedback is logged in `thumbs/feedback.md`, which the next video's thumbnails read. Also works on an existing video's thumbnails ("polish the thumbnails").

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-19)

Shorts from your long videos.

- **Shorts from a long video**: once a video is approved, the skill proposes 5 standalone moments, you pick 3, and each becomes a 30–55 s vertical Short (1080×1920) with its own `[HOOK] [PAYOFF] [LOOP]` script, rendered by the same pipeline in the same look and voice (`"format": "short"` in its `style.json`, or `render-video.mjs --vertical`). Text stays in the Shorts safe zone, pictures keep their 16:9 drawing in a band under the text (`focus`, `fit`), captions are burned in 2–4 words at a time (`"captions": false` turns them off), and `script-stats.mjs` and `youtube-meta.mjs` check a Short's own rules (length, a quick first line, no chapters, the long video linked). New `references/shorts-playbook.md`.
- `out/timeline.json` with every render: each slide's section, start, length and voice takes, to find a moment or cut a clip by time.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-18)

First public release.

- Topic to finished video, 8–20 minutes long (asked per video): a typed topic, a row from your topic sheet, or a brainstorm from a CSV; script in a fixed structure with subscribe asks, slides with a local AI voiceover (Kokoro), subtitles, real chapter times, titles and description, two A/B thumbnails.
- Beats: a longer pause (≈ 0.6 s) after the hook's promise, lines marked `> BEAT` and every section's conclusion, so viewers take each chunk in; `"beat": 0` turns them off.
- `youtube.json` with every upload field (title, description with chapters, tags, category, language, made-for-kids and altered-content answers, thumbnails, subtitles, pinned comment), checked against YouTube's limits by `youtube-meta.mjs` (local; nothing is uploaded).
- Optional research step before scripting: findings in `research.md` with a numbered, checkable source list the user approves; the script and the description cite it.
- Language asked per video (English or Spanish for now): script, slides, voices, subtitles, titles, description and thumbnails follow it; the renderer stops if the voice speaks another language.
- First-run setup writes `faceless-youtube.config.json` and a **channel kit** in your project: `channel/` with the brief (`channel.md`), your logo and avatar, and `youtube-settings.md` (description, keywords and upload defaults to paste into YouTube Studio). The scripts find `channel/logo.*` and `channel/avatar.*` by themselves; an older `./channel.md` still works.
- Looks: `clean` (your palette, fonts and logo), `paint-explainer` (MS Paint-style hand drawings), `sketch-explainer` (pencil and ink on warm paper) and `editorial` (serif headlines, data first), each with a `preview.png`; new looks from an inspiration folder. `theme.paper` sets an exact slide background.
- Optional paid voices: `"voice": "openai:<voice>"` (OpenAI speech API, with `voiceInstructions`) or `"elevenlabs:<voice id>"`, keys from the environment or the project's `.env`; same cache and pause trimming as Kokoro, which stays the free default.
- Doodles take the look's palette outside the paint look, and no longer draw words (they'd be in one language only).
- Spanish videos get Spanish chrome too: section labels, chapter cards ("Capítulo n"), the "n de N" counter, chapter titles and the Subscribe button.
- Thumbnail layouts in styles common on the home feed (`question`, `title`, `mood`, `card`) over the same scenes as the slides; Anton and Oswald bundled.
- Pace 1–5, optional animations (entrances, count-ups, self-drawing doodles, acting characters), `scene` slides with a mascot, people, robots, 8 backgrounds and props.
- Optional generated illustrations with the OpenAI Image API.
- Illustrated worlds and the `illustrated-explainer` look: layered vector backgrounds (`aurora`, `sea`, `dawn`) with depth, a `camera` that drifts or pushes through scene and illustration slides with parallax (`--camera`, style `camera`, or per cue), `fx` particles (snow, stars, dust, bokeh, sparkle…), `marks` (pins, rings, self-drawing paths, arrows, tags), and layered `illustration`s (`layers`: an opaque backdrop plus transparent cut-outs, generated or local files) with the mascot acting inside them. Theme `outline` and the `soft` texture for flat-vector looks.
- Viewer level, asked first (0–5): how technical the target viewer is. It recommends the length and sets the vocabulary, the everyday comparisons, the depth, the proof and the titles (script playbook › Viewer level); `script-stats.mjs` counts the technical terms the voice says and warns when they don't fit the level, or the length doesn't.
- `flow` diagrams: boxes and arrows laid out from a short list (chains, branches and merges, cycles, a hub with its parts), labelled arrows that draw themselves with a dot running along them, built one box per spoken line with `reveal: "build"`, optionally over an illustrated world.
- Characters: thinking shows as a thought cloud (no more loose white circles), and the mascot's arms are drawn over its body instead of peeking out as white slivers.
- The thumbnail compare sheet always shows the configured channel name; a `"channel"` in the spec is ignored with a warning.
- Working from a clone: `.gitignore` keeps your own files out of git (`faceless-youtube.config.json`, the `channel/` kit, `youtube-topics.csv`, `videos/`, `.env*`, `*.local.*` and your own looks named `styles/my-<name>/`), and the README shows how to copy the samples, so `git pull` updates the skill without touching your channel.
- `references/video-ideas.example.csv`: 12 example video ideas outlined in the script structure (hook, intro, ideas with claim · proof · catch, mid hook, summary, outro), with titles and two thumbnail angles each, across viewer levels 0–4, 8–18 minutes and two non-AI channels.
- Thumbnail messaging angles: each variant takes one angle (`explain`, `desire`, `fear`, `curiosity`, `proof`, `contrarian`, `identity`, `news`) that sets both its words and its picture, and A and B test two different angles by default (thumbnail playbook › Pick the angles first). New optional `angle` field in the thumbnail spec, a note the renderer ignores.
- Optional Zentesimal integration: topics from real customer questions.
- Emotion in the voice: `> TONE <name>` before a spoken line (`intrigue`, `suspense`, `surprise`, `excited`, `serious`, `warm`, `playful`) gives it its own delivery: a held pause before it, its own speed and pause cap, and for paid voices the acting itself (OpenAI per-line instructions on top of a default engaging-narrator one; ElevenLabs expressive settings and `eleven_v3` audio tags). `"expressive"` in `style.json` (or `--expressive`) scales it, 0 = off. The script playbook's new "Emotion and delivery" section gives the arc per section and the writing moves; `script-stats.mjs` counts the tones in its estimate and warns about a hook, idea or mid hook with no emotional turn, suspense that never pays off, a flat read or over-acting.
- The setup questions are a gate: `script-stats.mjs` fails a script whose `style.json` lacks the level, language, length, look, pace or voice (`"format": "presenter"` needs only the first three).
- Free or OpenAI, asked per video before the voice: the free local voice and vector scenes, or an OpenAI voice that acts the tones plus generated illustrations (or either one alone). `scripts/providers.mjs` reports whether the keys are there (never their values); `voice-samples.mjs --provider openai` plays the OpenAI candidates (`coral`, `sage`, `onyx`) in the video's language. `illustrations` joins the setup gate.
- Keys and `.env`: every script reads its variables through `scripts/env.mjs` in one fixed order (the environment, then the project's `.env`), with full `.env` syntax (`export`, quotes, comments); `references/env.example` lists every variable. First-run setup adds `.env` to `.gitignore`.
- `providers.mjs --check` tests that the OpenAI / ElevenLabs keys work and can use the configured models (free), `--live` adds one tiny paid request to confirm credit, and the voice and image scripts explain a failed request the same way (rejected key, model not allowed, organisation to verify, no credit, blocked host). New config field `models.speech` (default `gpt-4o-mini-tts`).
- `script-stats.mjs` runs the renderer's slide checks before any render: every `> SLIDE` is valid JSON on one line, no spoken line is the broken tail of a cue, each section opens with a slide, `reveal` lists have one line per item, and no slide stays up longer than the pace allows (same estimate and thresholds as `render-video.mjs`).
- `flow` diagrams with 6–7 boxes in a row stay readable: the arrow gap shrinks with the number of boxes, narrow boxes get compact padding and smaller text, every box in a diagram uses the same text size, and a word that still doesn't fit is reported instead of spilling over.
- Layered illustrations: a cut-out layer takes `x`, `y` and `scale` to place it in the frame (the image model always returns cut-outs centred), so rings and the cast can line up with it.
- First-run setup asks free or OpenAI once (saved as the default `video.voice` / `video.illustrations`), so the first video doesn't ask again, and asks for a profile picture or logo. With no topic sheet yet, topics are proposed from the channel's pillars instead of the shipped examples; "Brainstorm from a CSV" without a file offers pasted notes, keyword research or pillar ideas. The pace question recommends the chosen look's own pace.
- Estimates follow the chosen voice: `scripts/voices.mjs` holds each voice's real rate (Kokoro ≈ 185 words a minute at 0.92, OpenAI ≈ 170 at its new default speed 1.1), and `script-stats.mjs` and `render-video.mjs --slides` use it instead of assuming 185 (an OpenAI voice read 23 % slower, so scripts timed for Kokoro ran long and slides failed the pace check only after the voice was bought). The render prints the voice's real rate and warns when it drifts from the table.
- The full render stops right after the voice when a slide is far too long for the pace, instead of after filming every clip (minutes later); estimates count the slower delivery of suspense and serious lines.
- The voice cache is content-addressed (`video/voice/raw/<hash>.wav`): adding, moving or removing slides never re-speaks an unchanged line, and cloud takes are bought at their natural pace and sped up afterwards, so changing the speed doesn't buy them again. Progress is saved every 10 lines, so an interrupted run keeps what it bought.
- The look question is built from the `styles/` folder (`scripts/presets.mjs`: name, summary, branding, pace, previews), offering the best-fitting presets within the question's four options; any other preset can be named.
